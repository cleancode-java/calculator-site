How it works

This page explains how our UK tax calculator works, which tax thresholds and rates are used, and how to interpret the results shown in tables and charts.

The calculator follows official HMRC rules to estimate Income Tax, Class 4 National Insurance, and your Net Income.

Income Tax — how it is calculated

Income Tax in the UK is calculated using a progressive system. This means that different portions of your profit are taxed at different rates, depending on the official tax bands.

  • Personal Allowance (0%): £0 – £12,570
  • Basic Rate (20%): £12,571 – £50,270
  • Higher Rate (40%): £50,271 – £125,140
  • Additional Rate (45%): £125,141 and above

If your adjusted net income exceeds £100,000, your Personal Allowance is reduced. For every £2 over £100,000, your allowance decreases by £1. This creates a higher effective tax rate between £100,000 and £125,140.

Official source: HMRC Income Tax rates and allowances

Class 4 National Insurance — how it is calculated

If you are self-employed, you also pay Class 4 National Insurance contributions. These are calculated on your annual profit and are not affected by the Personal Allowance.

  • 0% on profits up to £12,570
  • 6% on profits from £12,571 to £50,270
  • 2% on profits above £50,270

Official source: HMRC Rates and allowances: National Insurance contributions HMRC National Insurance rates and categories

Understanding your results

After entering your income and expenses, the calculator displays your results using charts and a detailed table.

  • Pie Chart — shows how your total profit is split between Income Tax, National Insurance, and Net Income.
  • Bar Chart — compares your Profit, total taxes, and Net Income side by side.
  • Detailed table — breaks down each tax band, the taxable amount, and how much tax is due at each rate.

Why this matters for self-employed professionals

Understanding how tax and National Insurance are calculated helps you plan your finances, avoid surprises, and make informed decisions throughout the tax year.

This calculator is designed to support self-employed individuals (Class 4) by turning complex tax rules into clear numbers and visual insights.

These calculations are estimates based on current UK tax thresholds and rates. This tool does not replace an official HMRC calculation or professional tax advice.
