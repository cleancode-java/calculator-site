How allowable business expenses affect your tax

If you are self-employed, your tax is generally calculated based on your business profit rather than your total business income.

The calculator estimates your profit by subtracting your allowable business expenses from your business income:

Business income − Allowable business expenses = Business profit

Your estimated business profit is then used to calculate Income Tax and Class 4 National Insurance.

Only expenses that qualify as allowable business expenses should be entered into the calculator. The treatment of expenses can depend on your individual circumstances.

Income Tax — how it is calculated

Income Tax in the UK is calculated using a progressive system. This means that different portions of your profit are taxed at different rates, depending on the official tax bands.

  • Personal Allowance: up to £12,570 of income may be tax-free, depending on your circumstances.
  • Basic Rate (20%): applies to taxable income within the basic rate band.
  • Higher Rate (40%): applies to taxable income above the basic rate band.
  • Additional Rate (45%): applies to taxable income above £125,140.

If your adjusted net income exceeds £100,000, your Personal Allowance is reduced. For every £2 over £100,000, your allowance decreases by £1. This creates a higher effective tax rate between £100,000 and £125,140.

Official source: HMRC Income Tax rates and allowances

Class 4 National Insurance - how it is calculated

If you are self-employed, you also pay Class 4 National Insurance contributions. These are calculated on your annual profit and are not affected by the Personal Allowance.

  • 0% on profits up to £12,570
  • 6% on profits from £12,571 to £50,270
  • 2% on profits above £50,270
